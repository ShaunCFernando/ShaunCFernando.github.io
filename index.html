    • About Me!

    I graduated from University Scholars Program (USP), Pennsylvnia Leadership Charter School. I have completed 20 Advanced Placement (AP) classes with Differential Equations, Linear Algebra and Quantum Physics and earned a GPA of 4.889 in the Senior year. I enjoy exploring advanced topics in Physics and Calculus and am drawn toward a future in engineering. As a side note, I began Pre-Algebra in elementary school and completed Algebra I, Algebra II, Geometry, and Pre-Calculus (including Trigonometry) by 6th and 7th grade. My first AP course was AP Calculus AB in 8th grade.

    I am a National Merit Scholarship Finalist for the Class of 2026, USTA College Scholarship winner, class of 2026, Outstanding Young Citizen Gold Medalist (Trib-Total media) and a Semifinalist for both the Coca-Cola Scholars Program and the Elks Most Valuable Student Scholarship in 2026.

    Since my Montessori school years, I have been both gifted and curious. In my free time, I have explored different athletic pursuits, created my own algorithms to solve Rubik’s Cubes, challenged stronger chess players, and worked on addressing human-related environmental problems using robotics and machine learning. I am most passionate about playing the piano, though I have previously played the cello and French horn for fun.

    Currently

    Engineer by day, tennis competitor by weekends — spending Summer 2026 building my next robotics invention and playing UTR tournaments

    Previously

    Off the court I volunteered to help my non-profit tennis academy towards the mission to further expand access to tennis and mentorship for underserved youth. I have competed not only in the United States but also in Canada, Jamaica, Trinidad & Tobago, and Sri Lanka, where I gained valuable exposure to different cultures, people, and international playing styles. Most weekends, I have travel within US and overseas to compete in USTA, ITA, ITF and international tournaments, which I find especially rewarding because my hard work translates into results I am proud of. Tennis keeps me fit, happy, and strong and studies even suggest it can add up to ten years to our lives.

    When I first began building a surface water robot for testing and monitoring water contamination in public waterways and drinking sources, I joined EarthEcho International, whose mission aligned closely with mine. Since then, I have served as an Earth Echo Water Challenge Ambassador, contributing data to the national water quality database and working to raise community awareness about the importance of clean and safe water. Through library events, advocacy in forums such as OceanEcho 30x30, and hands-on community engagement, I strive to educate others on water preservation and inspire action toward environmental sustainability.

    I worked as a research intern at the University of Pittsburgh’s Hillman Academy Youth Education in Science (YES) program for two consecutive years. During this time, I applied my passion for coding and machine learning by developing algorithms to support biomedical research focused on cancer drug discovery.

    This work earned me recognition as one of the Top 300 National Broadcom MASTERS Innovators, distinguishing me among leading young researchers nationally and internationally.

    So What?

    My advice to anyone reading this is simple: follow your passion, trust the process, and do not focus solely on outcomes. In the end, passion in what you do leads to success and helps shape a better world and beyond. Thank you for visiting.
